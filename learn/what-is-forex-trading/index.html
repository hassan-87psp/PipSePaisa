What Is Forex Trading?

Forex trading is the buying of one currency and the simultaneous selling of another. The goal is to understand how exchange rates move and manage risk while participating in the global foreign-exchange market.

How the Forex Market Works

The foreign-exchange market, commonly called Forex or FX, is a global market where currencies are exchanged. Instead of buying a single asset by itself, a Forex trade is quoted as a pair such as EUR/USD, GBP/USD or USD/JPY. The first currency is the base currency and the second is the quote currency. If EUR/USD rises, one euro is becoming worth more US dollars; if it falls, the euro is becoming worth fewer US dollars.

Why Currency Prices Move

Exchange rates react to supply and demand. That supply and demand can be influenced by interest-rate expectations, inflation, employment data, economic growth, political events, central-bank communication and broader risk sentiment. Technical traders may also study market structure, support and resistance, candlesticks, liquidity and momentum to understand how price is behaving.

Forex Trading Sessions

Forex trades around the clock from Monday to Friday because major financial centres open at different times. Traders commonly refer to the Asian, London and New York sessions. Liquidity and volatility can change through the day, so a setup that behaves well during one session may behave differently during another. Learning session timing is part of building a repeatable trading process.

What Are Pips, Lots and Spread?

A pip is a standard way to describe a small price movement in many currency pairs. A lot describes trade size. The spread is the difference between the broker's bid and ask prices and is one of the costs of entering a trade. These concepts matter because a small-looking price move can have a very different financial impact depending on position size.

Leverage Does Not Remove Risk

Leverage allows a trader to control a larger market position with a smaller amount of account equity. It can increase potential gains, but it can also increase losses. Beginners should understand margin, stop loss, position sizing and maximum acceptable loss before focusing on potential profit.

A Better Learning Order for Beginners

  • Understand currency pairs and how prices are quoted.
  • Learn market sessions and basic economic drivers.
  • Study market structure, support and resistance and candlesticks.
  • Learn risk management and position sizing before increasing trade size.
  • Use a written trading plan and review trades consistently.

Technical and Fundamental Analysis

Technical analysis studies price behaviour and chart structure. Fundamental analysis studies economic data, monetary policy and market drivers. Many traders use one approach more heavily than the other, while others combine them. PipSePaisa provides structured learning around both areas through its Forex courses and mentor education.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Forex trading the same as gambling?

Forex is a financial market, but trading without a tested process, risk controls or understanding can become gambling-like behaviour. A disciplined plan and risk management are essential.

Can a beginner start with technical analysis?

Yes. Beginners can start with market structure, support and resistance, candlesticks and risk management, then add more advanced concepts gradually.
