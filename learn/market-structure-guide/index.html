Forex Market Structure: HH, HL, BOS and CHoCH

Understand how swing highs and lows create price structure, how traders label continuation and possible reversals, and why confirmation and risk planning are essential.

What Market Structure Means

Market structure describes how price moves from one meaningful high or low to the next. It is a practical way of reading a chart rather than predicting the future. Traders use it to organise price action into trends, ranges and potential transitions. A sequence on an M1 chart can be ordinary noise when viewed from H1, so begin by deciding which timeframe provides the context.

Before marking a structure break, locate relevant swing highs (visible local peaks) and swing lows (visible local troughs). Try to use a consistent method rather than changing the definition whenever the latest price move contradicts your interpretation.

Higher Highs (HH) and Higher Lows (HL)

In an uptrend, price often forms a higher high and then holds a higher low before trying to rise again. Consider hypothetical prices: a rally from 100 to 105, a pullback to 102, another rally to 108, and a pullback to 104. The later peaks and troughs are above earlier ones, which gives bullish context.

This does not mean every pullback should be bought. A potential entry still depends on the distance to nearby resistance, the current session, the volatility and whether the idea has a sensible invalidation point.

Lower Highs (LH) and Lower Lows (LL)

In a downtrend, price often forms lower highs and lower lows. A market that falls from 108 to 103, rebounds only to 106, then falls below 103 is showing weakening rebounds and another low. This is bearish structure, but the interpretation may change if the market enters a range.

When price repeatedly oscillates between similar highs and lows, it is safer to describe consolidation than to force bullish or bearish labels onto every small move.

Break of Structure (BOS)

A Break of Structure generally refers to price breaking a relevant prior swing in the direction of the established trend. In an uptrend, moving beyond a previous swing high can be called a bullish BOS; in a downtrend, moving beneath a prior swing low can be called a bearish BOS.

Some chart methods require a candle to close past the level rather than just wick through it. A wick that briefly takes a prior high and immediately rejects can tell a different story from a sustained break with follow-through. Use one clear rule so your analysis can be tested.

Change of Character (CHoCH)

A Change of Character is a label often used when price challenges the previous structural direction. For example, a clear break beneath an important higher low after a series of higher highs could warn that the bullish pattern is weakening.

CHoCH is not a guaranteed reversal signal. It may be the beginning of a deeper pullback or consolidation rather than an enduring new trend. Check higher-timeframe structure, nearby zones and the quality of the reaction before changing your trading bias.

Is Market Structure Part of Price Action or SMC?

The highs, lows and structural breaks are observable parts of price action. Smart Money Concepts (SMC) also uses terms such as BOS and CHoCH as part of a broader framework that studies price behaviour and liquidity. The same chart may be described with different labels, but traders should still be able to explain which price level matters and why.

How to Practise Marking Structure

  1. Start on H1 or H4 and mark obvious swings, not every candle.
  2. Describe the current sequence as HH/HL, LH/LL or a range.
  3. Mark the relevant swing point that could indicate a BOS or a possible CHoCH.
  4. Check nearby supply and demand zones.
  5. Use M5 or M1 for finer analysis only after the broader picture is clear.
  6. Wait for a defined reaction or candle close and plan your risk and invalidation before entering.
  7. Record both false breaks and valid-looking setups that fail.

No Confirmation = No Trade. No market-structure label guarantees a successful trade.

Common Market Structure Errors

  • Calling every tiny peak a meaningful swing high.
  • Using a low timeframe against clear higher-timeframe context without a plan.
  • Confusing a single wick with a confirmed structural break.
  • Assuming a CHoCH guarantees a lasting reversal.
  • Changing the marking rules only after seeing the outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can BOS and CHoCH guarantee Forex profits?

No. They describe the relationship between price and previous swing points, not a guaranteed outcome.

Which timeframe should beginners use?

H1 or H4 can provide useful context. Lower timeframes can be studied later for more detailed confirmations.

Is market structure the same as SMC?

Market structure is part of general price action, while SMC uses structure concepts within a broader trading framework.
