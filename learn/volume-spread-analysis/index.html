Volume Spread Analysis (VSA): A Practical Forex Guide

Understand how traders read the relationship between volume, candle range and closing prices, including Signs of Strength (SOS), Signs of Weakness (SOW) and the limits of volume data in Forex markets.

What Is Volume Spread Analysis?

Volume Spread Analysis (VSA) is a chart-reading approach that studies three connected observations: how much trading activity is reported, how wide a price bar or candle is, and where that candle closes relative to its high and low. Analysts use those clues to develop hypotheses about buying and selling pressure. VSA is a method of interpreting available evidence, not a way to identify every institutional order or predict price with certainty.

A wide-range candle that closes near its high can show strong upward movement, but the meaning changes depending on its volume, prior trend, nearby supply and subsequent candles. The same candle at major resistance may be interpreted differently from one that follows a long consolidation.

The Three Basic Elements of VSA

  1. Volume: reported activity during the bar. Unusually high or low readings are more meaningful when compared with recent bars on the same symbol and data source.
  2. Spread: the high-to-low range of the candle. A wide range shows greater price travel than a narrow range over the same interval.
  3. Close location: a candle ending near its high, low or middle can help describe the balance of buying and selling during that bar.

None of these elements is reliable in isolation. A trader needs market context, a consistent definition of relative volume and confirmation from later price behaviour.

Signs of Strength (SOS)

SOS is a VSA term for behaviour that may suggest demand is becoming more influential. Examples sometimes discussed in VSA education include effective upward progress following an area of support, or selling attempts that fail to push prices significantly lower. Whether a pattern represents genuine strength depends on where it occurs and what happens next.

In practice, a trader might first identify a relevant support or demand area, observe the candle's range and close, then wait for price to reclaim a nearby structure point. An SOS label alone is not a reason to enter a buy trade.

Signs of Weakness (SOW)

SOW refers to behaviour that may indicate selling pressure or insufficient demand. For instance, a market that struggles to advance at resistance despite high activity can warrant investigation. But activity without meaningful progress also has other possible explanations; it does not prove distribution or guarantee a downward reversal.

A more disciplined approach is to mark the important level, wait for bearish price confirmation and decide where a trade idea would be invalidated. Reading SOW together with market structure is generally more informative than treating an isolated bar as a signal.

Does Volume Spread Analysis Work in Spot Forex?

Spot Forex is decentralized. Many retail trading platforms display tick volume (the frequency of quote updates) rather than an exchange-wide count of every contract traded. It can still describe activity from that particular feed, but it should not be described as complete global Forex volume.

Exchange-traded futures may provide different volume and Open Interest information. Always check the asset class, data provider and indicator inputs before comparing one chart's VSA readings with another. Gold, Forex pairs and futures contracts can produce different interpretations because their volume data differ.

How VSA Connects With Market Structure and Time Fractals

Market structure gives VSA an important location and directional context. A suspected SOS near a higher-timeframe demand area carries a different narrative from a similar candle directly beneath resistance. Traders can begin by marking H1 or H4 structure and then study more detailed reactions on lower timeframes, without assuming that lower-timeframe signals override the broader context.

The term time fractal is used in some trading approaches to describe patterns or price behaviour examined across multiple timeframes. Methods differ, so the practical goal is to define which timeframe establishes context, which provides a setup and which provides confirmation.

COT Reports and Open Interest

The Commitments of Traders (COT) reports published by the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission summarize reportable futures and options positions. They are periodic and delayed; they are better suited to broader positioning context than to precise intraday trade entries. Categories and coverage vary by report.

Open Interest is the number of outstanding derivative contracts that have not been closed or settled. Changes in Open Interest can complement price and futures-volume analysis, but do not reveal a trade's success or guarantee the next price direction. Use the correct contract and reporting source when comparing data.

A Practical VSA Analysis Workflow

  1. Choose the instrument and verify what its reported volume measures.
  2. Mark higher-timeframe support, resistance and market structure.
  3. Compare current volume and candle range with a consistent recent sample.
  4. Ask whether the candle's close and location support an SOS or SOW hypothesis.
  5. Wait for a meaningful candle close, retest or another clearly defined confirmation.
  6. Plan the invalidation level, position size and maximum acceptable loss before considering a trade.
  7. Review both successful and failed-looking patterns in a trading journal.

No Confirmation = No Trade. VSA is a method for organising observations, not a guarantee. Use a demo account for learning and avoid increasing position size to recover losses.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming every high-volume candle represents buying by large institutions.
  • Comparing volume from unrelated providers or different contract months without adjustment.
  • Ignoring higher-timeframe structure and nearby support or resistance.
  • Entering before the candle closes or treating SOS and SOW labels as certain signals.
  • Using COT reports as if they were real-time order-flow information.
  • Planning the entry but not the stop-loss or risk limit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are SOS and SOW in VSA?

SOS stands for Signs of Strength, while SOW stands for Signs of Weakness. They describe possible conditions, not guaranteed trades.

Is tick volume the same as complete Forex market volume?

No. Tick volume typically reflects quote-update activity from a particular feed; decentralized spot Forex does not have one comprehensive centralized volume tape.

Can COT reports predict the next Gold or Forex candle?

No. COT data is periodic and delayed, so it is better used as part of broader positioning context rather than an immediate entry signal.
