Margin and Free Margin in Forex: Equity, Margin Level and Stop-Out

PipSePaisa • Forex Education & Research

INTRODUCTION Margin is collateral required to hold a leveraged position, not a purchase price or a direct measure of maximum loss. Understanding margin, equity and margin level helps traders avoid unintentionally running out of room when markets move against open positions. 1. KEY TERMS Balance is cash after closed transactions. Equity usually equals balance plus floating profit or loss and applicable adjustments. Used margin is collateral assigned to open positions. Free margin commonly equals equity minus used margin. Broker terminology and calculations can vary. 2. MARGIN LEVEL A common platform formula is margin level (%) = equity / used margin × 100, when used margin is positive. For example, $800 equity with $400 used margin gives 200%. At $500 equity and $400 used margin, the level falls to 125%. 3. MARGIN CALL VS STOP-OUT A margin call is a warning or requirement tied to insufficient margin. Stop-out may automatically close positions when the broker's minimum level is breached. The exact thresholds, liquidation sequence and protections depend on broker and product rules. 4. WORKED POSITION EXAMPLE Suppose the account begins with $1,000, opens positions using $250 margin and currently shows a $100 floating loss. Ignoring charges, equity is $900, free margin $650 and margin level 360%. A larger adverse price move decreases equity and free margin even if the position has not yet closed. 5. WHY LEVERAGE CAN CONFUSE At higher leverage, opening a large position may require less margin, making available free margin look generous. But the market exposure remains large. Losses depend on the notional exposure and actual price movement, not just initial collateral. 6. RISK CHECKLIST Check contract value, planned SL loss, aggregate exposure and existing floating P&L. Leave room for spreads and volatility. Do not open another trade just because the 'free margin' number is positive. 7. COMMON MISTAKES Treating account balance as available equity, confusing used margin with acceptable risk and relying on broker liquidation instead of planning stops. FAQ Can free margin be negative? Depending on platform and market moves, yes. Is a margin call a guaranteed safety net? No. MARGIN IS NOT A LOSS LIMIT Margin is collateral required to maintain a leveraged position. It is not a trading fee and not a maximum loss guarantee. A platform may allow an order because margin is available even though the Stop Loss, if hit, would exceed the trader's intended monetary risk. BALANCE, EQUITY, USED MARGIN, FREE MARGIN Balance reflects closed account transactions. Equity usually includes floating profit or loss. Used Margin is collateral assigned to open positions. Free Margin generally equals Equity minus Used Margin. The exact calculations and credits differ by broker. Always monitor Equity, not merely Balance, when positions are open. MARGIN LEVEL EXAMPLE A typical Margin Level formula is Equity ÷ Used Margin × 100. If an account has $1,000 balance, $250 used margin and $100 floating loss, then Equity is approximately $900, Free Margin $650 and Margin Level 360%, ignoring additional adjustments. A further adverse market move reduces Equity and Free Margin. MARGIN CALL AND STOP-OUT Brokers define minimum margin requirements and the conditions for warning or automatic liquidation. They may close positions if Equity drops too far relative to required collateral. Stop-out execution is not necessarily instant at an exact amount and may not prevent a larger loss during gaps. WHY HIGH LEVERAGE IS DECEPTIVE Higher permitted leverage can reduce collateral needed to open the same notional exposure, which makes the Free Margin figure appear generous. But market losses on the position do not automatically become smaller. To assess risk, calculate Stop Loss distance times tick/pip value times volume. MULTIPLE POSITIONS Opening another trade because there is Free Margin can create concentrated risk. Existing Gold and USD-pair positions may respond together to a central-bank surprise. Calculate combined planned loss and leave operational buffer for normal spread changes. FAQ Can Equity be lower than Balance? Yes, with floating losses. Is positive Free Margin permission to risk more? No. Will broker Stop-Out save the entire deposit? No guarantee. What matters most? Exposure, planned loss and equity. BALANCE IS NOT THE SAME AS TRADING EQUITY Balance typically includes completed deposits, withdrawals and realized trade results. Equity incorporates floating gains and losses while trades remain open, subject to broker accounting. If an account displays $1,000 Balance but an open trade has -$180 unrealized P&L, the relevant Equity may be about $820, before other adjustments. A trader who only monitors Balance can miss a deteriorating risk situation. USED MARGIN IS COLLATERAL Used Margin refers to collateral tied to open exposure. It is not equivalent to a paid commission and it does not set the maximum possible loss. Depending on broker rules, offsetting or hedged positions may have special margin treatments. Contract size, symbol leverage and account type all affect required collateral. Read the broker's margin calculation for XAUUSD and other CFDs rather than assuming standard EUR/USD rules apply. FREE MARGIN AND WHAT IT MEANS Free Margin is commonly calculated as Equity less Used Margin. It represents capacity that may be available for additional positions or for absorbing some adverse movement. It is not cash that can be treated as money already earned. An account can have positive Free Margin while holding positions with an unacceptable potential stop-out loss. The ability to open a trade is not evidence that you should. MARGIN LEVEL PERCENTAGE A typical formula is Margin Level (%) = Equity ÷ Used Margin × 100 whenever Used Margin is greater than zero. Suppose Equity is $900 and Used Margin is $300; Margin Level is 300%. If the floating loss worsens and Equity drops to $450 while Used Margin stays $300, the level falls to 150%. Broker-specific warnings and automatic liquidation thresholds are separate from this arithmetic. A COMPLETE ACCOUNT EXAMPLE Start with $1,500 Balance. Open positions requiring $400 Used Margin. If floating P&L is -$200, then Equity is approximately $1,300 and Free Margin $900, ignoring other adjustments. Margin Level is about 325%. Another $300 adverse move in floating P&L would leave Equity near $1,000 and Free Margin $600. The platform may still permit further exposure, but that does not make it consistent with a conservative risk plan. MARGIN CALL AND STOP-OUT DIFFER A Margin Call can be a warning or collateral requirement when a threshold is reached. Stop-Out often refers to broker-driven liquidation when the account violates lower margin requirements. The levels and sequence vary across jurisdictions, account products and providers. In fast markets, fills may be poor or positions may close at unexpected prices. Treat these processes as last-resort account mechanics, not as primary Stop Loss placement. WHY LEVERAGE MAKES FREE MARGIN MISLEADING A broker offering a high leverage ratio may require relatively little margin for a large notional position. The Free Margin number can therefore look comfortable while the full price exposure remains substantial. If the market moves adversely, floating loss can destroy Equity rapidly. Always calculate pip-based or price-unit-based money risk independently from the collateral requirement. MULTIPLE OPEN POSITIONS Suppose a trader opens EUR/USD Buy, USD/CHF Sell and XAUUSD Buy. These positions may share sensitivity to US-dollar expectations. Even though each margin requirement appears manageable, simultaneous adverse movement can reduce Equity across all positions at once. Review both aggregate margin and the total monetary loss at stops under a correlated scenario. HEDGING DOES NOT ELIMINATE COST Opening opposite positions can alter net market exposure but may still involve two spreads, financing costs or complex broker margin treatment. A Buy and Sell on the same symbol is not guaranteed to produce a risk-free account. Pricing conventions, contract types and execution risk remain. Do not assume a hedge creates free profit or that the broker will always treat opposite tickets as fully offset. HOW TO AVOID FORCED LIQUIDATION Define the structural Stop Loss first, calculate proper lot size and maintain an operational margin buffer. Avoid layering extra trades solely because there is unused buying power. Watch upcoming CPI, NFP and FOMC events, where widening spreads and rapid P&L changes can erode margin. Review overnight financing and market-opening gap risk for multi-day positions. COMMON INTERPRETATION ERRORS A platform Balance of $2,000 with Equity $1,200 is not a fully intact $2,000 account when floating losses exist. A high Margin Level does not automatically imply that a trade has positive expectancy. Used Margin is not the same as risk-to-stop. A broker Stop-Out is not a guaranteed floor under realized losses. FAQ Can Free Margin fall while no trade is closed? Yes, floating losses reduce Equity. Can Margin Level fluctuate minute by minute? Yes. Are Margin Calls identical across brokers? No. Is high leverage a method to create more profit safely? No, it increases capacity for exposure. Should traders wait for Stop-Out to control losses? No. CONCLUSION Use equity and risk budgets to manage trades; margin metrics are an additional operational warning system.
