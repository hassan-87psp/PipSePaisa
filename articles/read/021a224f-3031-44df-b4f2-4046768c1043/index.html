What Is a Currency Pair in Forex? Major, Minor & Exotic Pairs Explained

PipSePaisa • Forex Education & Research

A currency pair represents the value of one currency compared with another currency. In forex trading, currencies are always traded in pairs because when you buy one currency, you are simultaneously selling another. For example: EUR/USD GBP/USD USD/JPY Each pair contains two currencies: a base currency and a quote currency. What Is a Currency Pair? A currency pair shows how much of the second currency is needed to buy one unit of the first currency. Take EUR/USD as an example. EUR = Base Currency USD = Quote Currency If EUR/USD is trading at 1.1700, it means that 1 Euro is worth approximately 1.17 US Dollars. The first currency in the pair is always called the base currency, while the second is called the quote currency. Base Currency and Quote Currency Understanding the difference between the base and quote currency is one of the first things a beginner should learn. In GBP/USD: GBP is the base currency. USD is the quote currency. If GBP/USD rises, it generally means the British Pound is strengthening relative to the US Dollar, or the US Dollar is weakening relative to the Pound. If GBP/USD falls, it generally means the British Pound is weakening relative to the US Dollar, or the US Dollar is strengthening relative to the Pound. Why Are Currencies Traded in Pairs? Currencies do not have a meaningful value by themselves in the forex market. Their value is measured relative to another currency. For example, saying that the Euro is “strong” does not provide enough information. The important question is: Strong compared with which currency? The Euro may strengthen against the US Dollar while weakening against the British Pound at the same time. This is why forex prices are expressed as currency pairs. What Are Major Currency Pairs? Major currency pairs are among the most actively traded pairs in the forex market. They all include the US Dollar together with another major global currency. Common major currency pairs include: EUR/USD – Euro / US Dollar GBP/USD – British Pound / US Dollar USD/JPY – US Dollar / Japanese Yen USD/CHF – US Dollar / Swiss Franc AUD/USD – Australian Dollar / US Dollar USD/CAD – US Dollar / Canadian Dollar NZD/USD – New Zealand Dollar / US Dollar Major pairs usually have high trading activity and liquidity. Because they are actively traded, they often have tighter spreads compared with less liquid currency pairs, although trading conditions vary between brokers and market conditions. What Are Minor Currency Pairs? Minor currency pairs, sometimes called cross-currency pairs, generally involve major currencies but do not include the US Dollar. Examples include: EUR/GBP EUR/JPY GBP/JPY EUR/AUD GBP/AUD AUD/JPY For example, EUR/GBP compares the Euro directly with the British Pound. Minor pairs can still have significant trading activity, but their spreads and volatility may differ from the major pairs. What Are Exotic Currency Pairs? Exotic currency pairs normally combine a major currency with the currency of a smaller or emerging economy. Examples may include: USD/TRY – US Dollar / Turkish Lira USD/ZAR – US Dollar / South African Rand USD/MXN – US Dollar / Mexican Peso EUR/TRY – Euro / Turkish Lira Exotic pairs can behave differently from major currency pairs. They may have: Lower liquidity Wider spreads Higher volatility Greater sensitivity to local economic or political developments Because of these characteristics, exotic currency pairs may involve additional trading risk. How Do Currency Pair Prices Move? Currency pair prices change according to the relative strength and demand of the two currencies. For example, EUR/USD can rise when demand for the Euro increases relative to the US Dollar. It can fall when the US Dollar becomes stronger relative to the Euro. Many factors can influence these movements, including: Interest rates Inflation Employment reports Economic growth Central bank decisions Political developments Market sentiment Supply and demand This is why forex traders often study both technical analysis and fundamental analysis. What Does Buy Mean in a Currency Pair? When you buy a currency pair, you are effectively buying the base currency and selling the quote currency. For example: Buy EUR/USD You are taking a position based on the expectation that the Euro may strengthen relative to the US Dollar. If EUR/USD rises after your entry, the trade may move in your favour. What Does Sell Mean in a Currency Pair? When you sell a currency pair, you are effectively selling the base currency and buying the quote currency. For example: Sell EUR/USD You are taking a position based on the expectation that the Euro may weaken relative to the US Dollar. If EUR/USD falls after your entry, the trade may move in your favour. However, every position involves risk, and the market can move in the opposite direction. Which Currency Pairs Are Best for Beginners? There is no single currency pair that is automatically best for every trader. However, beginners often start by studying major currency pairs because they generally have high liquidity and are widely followed by market participants. Examples include: EUR/USD GBP/USD USD/JPY Instead of monitoring many pairs at once, a beginner may benefit from learning how a small number of pairs behave during different market sessions and economic conditions. What Affects Different Currency Pairs? Different currency pairs can react to different economic factors. For example: EUR/USD may react strongly to decisions from the US Federal Reserve and European Central Bank. GBP/USD may be influenced by the Bank of England, UK inflation data and US economic developments. USD/JPY can be influenced by Federal Reserve policy, Bank of Japan decisions and changes in global risk sentiment. AUD/USD may react to Australian economic data, commodity trends and developments involving major trading partners. Understanding the currencies behind a pair can help traders understand why its price may be moving. Major vs Minor vs Exotic Currency Pairs The main difference between these categories is the currencies involved and the level of market activity. Major pairs include the US Dollar and another major currency. Minor pairs normally contain two major currencies without the US Dollar. Exotic pairs usually combine a major currency with a currency from a smaller or emerging economy. Each category can have different levels of liquidity, spreads and volatility. Final Thoughts Understanding currency pairs is one of the most important foundations of forex trading. Every forex trade involves two currencies, and traders are always analysing the value of one currency relative to another. Before placing trades, beginners should understand: Base Currency Quote Currency Major Currency Pairs Minor Currency Pairs Exotic Currency Pairs Buy and Sell positions Factors that influence currency prices Once currency pairs are properly understood, concepts such as pips, lot sizes, spreads, leverage and market analysis become much easier to learn. Common major pairs: EUR/USD – Euro / US Dollar GBP/USD – British Pound / US Dollar USD/JPY – US Dollar / Japanese Yen USD/CHF – US Dollar / Swiss Franc AUD/USD – Australian Dollar / US Dollar USD/CAD – US Dollar / Canadian Dollar NZD/USD – New Zealand Dollar / US Dollar Major pairs me generally liquidity zyada hoti hai. Minor currency pairs ko cross-currency pairs bhi kaha jata hai. Inme major currencies hoti hain, lekin US Dollar include nahi hota. Examples: EUR/GBP EUR/JPY GBP/JPY EUR/AUD GBP/AUD AUD/JPY For example, EUR/GBP directly Euro ko British Pound ke against compare karta hai. Minor pairs me bhi achi trading activity ho sakti hai, lekin unki volatility aur spread major pairs se different ho sakti hai. Exotic Currency Pairs Kya Hote Hain? Exotic currency pairs normally ek major currency ko kisi smaller ya emerging economy ki currency ke sath combine karte hain. Inme aksar: Liquidity kam ho sakti hai Spread zyada ho sakta hai Volatility high ho sakti hai Local political ya economic news ka impact zyada ho sakta hai Isi liye exotic currency pairs me trading risk bhi comparatively zyada ho sakta hai. Currency Pair Ki Price Kaise Move Karti Hai? Currency pair ki price dono currencies ki relative strength aur demand ke according move karti hai. For example: Agar Euro ki demand US Dollar ke comparison me increase hoti hai, to EUR/USD upar ja sakta hai. Agar US Dollar Euro ke against strong hota hai, to EUR/USD niche aa sakta hai. Currency prices ko bohat se factors affect karte hain: Interest rates Inflation Employment reports Economic growth Central bank decisions Political developments Market sentiment Supply aur demand Isi liye forex traders technical analysis aur fundamental analysis dono use karte hain. Currency Pair Mein Buy Ka Kya Matlab Hai? Jab aap currency pair buy karte hain, to effectively aap base currency buy aur quote currency sell kar rahe hote hain. Example: Buy EUR/USD Iska matlab hai ke aap expect kar rahe hain ke Euro US Dollar ke against strong ho sakta hai. Agar EUR/USD aapki entry ke baad upar jata hai, to trade aapki expected direction me move kar raha hota hai. Currency Pair Mein Sell Ka Kya Matlab Hai? Jab aap currency pair sell karte hain, to effectively aap base currency sell aur quote currency buy kar rahe hote hain. Example: Sell EUR/USD Iska matlab hai ke aap expect kar rahe hain ke Euro US Dollar ke against weak ho sakta hai. Agar EUR/USD aapki entry ke baad niche jata hai, to trade aapki expected direction me move karta hai. Lekin market opposite direction me bhi move kar sakti hai, is liye har trade me risk hota hai. Lekin beginners aksar major currency pairs se start karte hain, kyun ke inme liquidity generally high hoti hai aur inko market participants bohat closely follow karte hain. Examples: EUR/USD GBP/USD USD/JPY Beginner ke liye bohat saare pairs ek sath monitor karne ke bajaye 2 ya 3 pairs ko properly samajhna zyada useful ho sakta hai. Different Currency Pairs Ko Kya Affect Karta Hai? Har currency pair different economic factors se influence ho sakta hai. For example: EUR/USD Federal Reserve aur European Central Bank ke decisions se strongly react kar sakta hai. GBP/USD Bank of England, UK inflation data aur US economic developments se influence ho sakta hai. USD/JPY Federal Reserve, Bank of Japan aur global market sentiment se react kar sakta hai. AUD/USD Australian economic data, commodity trends aur global trade developments se affect ho sakta hai. Agar trader pair ke dono currencies ke fundamentals samajhta ho, to market movement ko samajhna easy ho jata hai.
