Risk-to-Reward Ratio in Forex: R Multiples, Win Rate and Expectancy

PipSePaisa • Forex Education & Research

INTRODUCTION Risk-to-reward ratio (R:R) compares the potential planned loss of a trade with its potential planned gain. It helps express whether a target offers sufficient space relative to the invalidation point. But ratio alone does not show probability, costs or profitability. 1. HOW TO CALCULATE IT For a long trade entered at 1.2000 with Stop Loss 1.1970 and Take Profit 1.2060, risk equals 30 pips and planned reward equals 60 pips. Written as risk:reward, the ratio is 1:2. A 45-pip target for the same 30-pip risk offers 1:1.5. 2. WHAT 'R' MEANS One R is the amount planned to lose if the stop is hit. If you risk $10, a result of +2R is approximately +$20 before trading costs, and -1R about -$10. Recording results in R makes positions of different monetary sizes easier to compare. 3. WIN RATE AND BREAKEVEN Ignoring costs, a strategy that earns +2R on wins and loses -1R on losses breaks even at a win rate of about 33.3%. With 1:1, the breakeven rate is 50%. Trading costs make the required win rate somewhat higher. 4. EXPECTANCY MATTERS Expected R per trade = (win probability × average win in R) − (loss probability × average loss in R) − costs. For example, 45% wins at 1.5R and 55% losses at 1R yield +0.125R gross expectancy. Small samples can be misleading, and real slippage changes the result. 5. MARKET STRUCTURE BEFORE TARGET Do not force a 1:3 target if major resistance sits directly ahead. The target must be plausible given actual structure and volatility. Likewise a large theoretical reward is not useful if the stop is tucked inside ordinary market noise. 6. PARTIAL EXITS AND BREAKEVEN Taking half off at +1R and the remainder at +2R yields +1.5R in total if both portions exit as planned. Moving the remainder to breakeven changes the distribution. Journal actual rather than advertised R multiples. 7. COMMON ERRORS Confusing reward:risk with risk:reward wording, assuming 1:3 always beats 1:1, overlooking commissions and choosing a stop only to manufacture an impressive ratio. FAQ Is 1:2 mandatory? No; optimal targets depend on validated strategy and market context. Can 70% win rate lose money? Yes, when average losses are much larger than wins. CALCULATING RISK TO REWARD For a long trade at EUR/USD 1.2000, SL 1.1970 and TP 1.2060, planned risk is 30 pips and reward is 60 pips. The risk:reward ratio is 1:2. For a Sell, the arithmetic is reversed: stop is above entry and target below. Spread, commission and slippage reduce net returns. WHAT IS AN R-MULTIPLE? One R equals the initial planned risk on a trade. If that risk is $10, a +2R winner yields about $20 gross while -1R means about $10 planned loss. Tracking results in R lets traders compare performance across different balances and lot sizes without confusing large positions with good decisions. BREAKEVEN WIN RATE Ignoring transaction costs, 1:1 average win/loss needs 50% wins to break even; 1:2 needs approximately 33.3%; 1:3 needs 25%. But strategies with wider targets may hit targets less often. Higher reward multiple alone does not mean better actual expectancy. EXPECTANCY EXAMPLE Suppose a system wins 45% of trades at an average +1.5R and loses 55% at -1R. Gross expectancy is 0.45×1.5 – 0.55×1 = +0.125R per trade before costs. If execution costs total 0.15R on average, net expectancy becomes negative. Therefore backtest fees and actual fills. REALISTIC TARGETS If H1 supply sits just 15 pips above a 30-pip-risk Buy, claiming a 1:3 target beyond supply is questionable. Identify the likely barriers to price before deciding whether the trade has an acceptable reward profile. PARTIAL EXITS CHANGE RR If half the position closes at +1R and half at +2R, total gain is +1.5R when both are reached. If the second half stops out at breakeven, the overall result differs. Journal fractional exits, fees and maximum favorable/adverse movement. FAQ Does 1:2 always beat 1:1? No. Does 70% win rate guarantee profit? No, average losses may dominate. Does RR measure risk probability? No, it only compares planned price distances. RISK:REWARD IS A PRICE-DISTANCE COMPARISON A Risk-to-Reward Ratio compares planned monetary downside with planned upside. A 1:2 trade risks one unit for a target of two units. It does not predict how frequently the target will be hit. A trader could mark any distant target on a chart; only a tested system and market structure can determine whether that target is plausible. Transaction costs reduce realized reward and may increase effective risk. BUY AND SELL EXAMPLES Buy EUR/USD at 1.1000, Stop Loss 1.0980 and Take Profit 1.1040: risk 20 pips and target 40 pips, a planned 1:2 before costs. Sell GBP/USD at 1.2750, SL 1.2780 and TP 1.2690: risk 30 pips and target 60 pips, also 1:2. These identical ratios can carry different money risks because the instrument's pip value and lot size differ. R MULTIPLE IS A UNIT OF INITIAL RISK If initial planned loss is $12, that amount is 1R. A realized +$24 gain is +2R before trading costs, and -$12 is -1R. Tracking R makes strategies easier to compare when account balances and position volumes change. But do not quietly redefine 1R after a trade has already moved; the initial risk reference should remain documented for honest performance analysis. BREAK-EVEN WIN RATE FORMULA Ignoring costs, a system that wins W units per win and loses L units per loss breaks even when win probability × W equals loss probability × L. At +2R average wins and -1R average losses, the breakeven win rate is 1/(2+1) ≈ 33.3%. At +1.5R versus -1R it is 1/(1.5+1)=40%. At +3R versus -1R it is 25%. Trading costs increase the required success rate. A STRATEGY WITH HIGH WIN RATE THAT LOSES Suppose 80% of positions win +0.3R but 20% lose -1.5R. Gross expectancy is 0.8×0.3 − 0.2×1.5 = -0.06R. Many green trades can disguise a losing strategy. A high win rate is not a substitute for a realistic payoff distribution. Compare average result in R and maximum drawdown, not just how frequently the broker terminal shows a profit. A STRATEGY WITH LOWER WIN RATE THAT WINS GROSS Suppose 42% win +2R and 58% lose -1R. Gross expectancy is 0.42×2 − 0.58×1 = +0.26R per trade. If average trading costs, slippage and other effects reduce outcome by 0.1R per trade, the simplified net estimate becomes +0.16R. In practice, costs may depend on trade duration and outcome, so measure actual realized trades rather than assuming a constant fee. RISK-REWARD AND MARKET STRUCTURE A potential Buy from H1 Demand may encounter previous H1 Supply before its distant +3R objective. If that Supply is a meaningful obstacle, a trader cannot merely ignore it because 1:3 looks attractive. A tested strategy may use partial exits or a nearer target, but changes should be applied consistently. The best ratio depends on the conditional setup, not a universal number. HOW STOP SIZE AFFECTS THE RATIO Moving a Stop Loss closer may improve the printed R:R without improving the trade's chance of success. If the original structural invalidation is 40 pips away, pretending risk is 10 pips may place the stop within normal fluctuations. The strategy then changes. A better process is to maintain structural invalidation and reduce position volume to respect monetary risk. PARTIAL TAKE PROFIT MATHEMATICS Suppose 25% of volume closes at +1R, 25% at +2R and 50% at +3R. If every target executes, total gross R is 0.25×1 + 0.25×2 + 0.50×3 = +2.25R. If the last half instead closes at breakeven, actual realized R is lower. Tracking individual target hits without weighting the volume can exaggerate strategy outcomes. BREAKEVEN MANAGEMENT CHANGES EXPECTANCY Moving Stop Loss to Entry at +1R might protect some capital after favorable movement but can turn would-be winners into near-zero trades if the market retests the entry zone. The net cost of a nominal Breakeven exit may remain negative after commissions. Compare a backtest with and without the breakeven rule instead of assuming it must improve profitability. WHY AVERAGES NEED A MEANINGFUL SAMPLE Ten trades can create a misleading win rate or expectancy. A strategy may behave differently during NFP weeks, quiet Asian sessions, strong trends and prolonged ranges. Assess enough observations across regimes, record confidence in your estimates and avoid treating backtest profitability as a promise. Drawdowns and losing streaks are part of the distribution, not anomalies to ignore. A PRACTICAL STRATEGY REVIEW SHEET Record total valid setups, actual traded setups, winners, losers, average winner in R, average loser in R, fees, expectancy, maximum drawdown and rule violations. Keep the plan's target method fixed during the review period. If you change from fixed 1:2 exits to multiple partial targets, treat the new rules as a different tested strategy rather than mixing the results without explanation. FAQ Is 1:2 always better than 1:1? No. Can a 30% win rate be profitable? It can under sufficient average payoff, after costs. Does the RR number include probability? No. Does a +3R screenshot mean the full position earned +3R? Not if earlier partial exits reduced volume. Should every setup be forced into a 1:3 target? No. CONCLUSION Evaluate ratio alongside setup quality, win probability, transaction costs and realistic execution. PIPSEPAISA FREE COURSES Sir Sajid Khan Ghori — Free Forex Technical Analysis Course.
